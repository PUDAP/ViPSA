# Continuation integrity: validated recovery lessons

## Native evidence, not controller labels

A disk-full interruption left a stale preparation-not-started status while native receipts proved forming and seven preparation operations completed. Audit every ordered native lifecycle and exact identity before choosing the first missing operation. Keep original journals immutable; append recovery evidence rather than replaying motion, forming or completed conditioning. Continuation energy may already include inherited energy: reconcile incremental exposure keys, not the sum of two cumulative totals.

## Due-health continuation and outcome separation

An expired owner context can stop before mandatory health dispatch despite a confirmed cycle-level recipe failure. Prove absence of the health INTENT and native dispatch, then use a one-shot, health-only backend with fresh ownership, schema/idle checks, OFF/open/errors and pose readbacks. Near-compliance health is a terminal safety stop, not grounds for retry. Preserve the original UNKNOWN interruption flag and known failure latch as separate fields. Original UNKNOWN alone does not justify stopping autonomous next-device progression once required terminal evidence is reconciled.

## Full terminal context binding

A tested validator gap admitted substituted terminal context because reconciliation omitted the complete context validator. The verified fix revalidates full context and binds its digest to the permanent one-shot claim, compiled recipe, historical audit and source journal. Regressions must include altered authority flags, changed proof hashes, and self-consistent replacement context/proof pairs. Checking launch authority alone is insufficient.

## Namespace and clock checks

Historical evidence inventories must distinguish exact completed-operation evidence from later legitimate read-only discovery/readiness receipts. Classify the latter through explicit command allowlists, lifecycle identities, ordered steps, hashes and schema bindings. Reject unexplained stress/motion, unknown INTENTs, corruption and historical mutation; never globally allow extra roots.

A validated timestamp correction checked request and reply sequences within their producer semantics instead of imposing a combined ordering across host clocks or differently rounded SDK timestamps. Preserve identity and sequence checks and test against actual retained native receipts; do not synthesize missing timestamps to claim freshness.

## Safe controller rebinding

Global replacement of a physical-device numeral changed a timestamp embedded in the campaign identifier. Completed native commands were retained under their exact recorded identifier and admitted only through a narrowly audited alias; history was not rewritten. For future controllers, rebind explicit AST integer literals or named KEY/START/FINAL parameters. Assert exact campaign frame and key, physical/recipe identity, numeric waveform parity and ordered native moves. Test preservation of timestamp strings containing the source device digits.

## Release and orchestration

Run a single persistent serial gate with immutable tested-source hashes and explicit results. Child timeouts can leave standalone tests running: inspect exact process identity and durable logs before duplication. Freeze only after every named test passes on unchanged source. Independently reverify before parent execution. Historical compiler parity can depend on interpreter numeric behavior; reproduce the tested runtime and diagnose exact differences rather than silently relaxing parity.

Complete slow historical audits before fresh owner attestation. At later stable boundaries, use new verified readiness when expiry requires it, not a wider TTL. Historical dispatch-time freshness never proves current pose. Standing user start/auto-next authorization does not need repeated approval, but cannot replace fresh native safety checks. Report readiness, navigation, acquisition and terminal outcome distinctly; deliver tested executable parent entries instead of documentation-only handoffs.
