# Terminal health accounting and returned-navigation recovery

## Known terminal health stop is not a replay boundary

A native health command may return a complete successful response yet correctly fail a near-compliance safety validator. Preserve the original UNKNOWN/error record and raw native reply; do not repeat the health read or apply additional stress. Independently verify output OFF, matrix open, error queue and final position.

When a prior interrupted run and its continuation exist, audit them together: exact original journal and claim hashes, full START/command/COMPLETE exchanges, contiguous completed cycles, recipe parameters, once-only forming/preparation, inherited failure latch and cumulative energy. A rejected health read that was not charged in the saved ledger is **uncharged exposure**, not zero energy. An additive terminal assessment can establish known exposure and cleanup while retaining historical UNKNOWN. It cannot waive unrelated uncertain events.

The exercised chain preserved 50 original cycles, resumed the missing health boundary once, completed cycles51–60, and then ended at a genuine returned-health compliance stop. Next-device progression used the fully audited original-plus-continuation chain, not a cleared UNKNOWN flag.

## Navigation timeout after successful returned motion

A foreground orchestration timeout can occur after all motion commands returned success but before final readback/cleanup bookkeeping. Do not rerun navigation from the beginning.

1. Inspect the durable operation row, ordered intents and responses. Match each response to the exact request/event, native machine/command/parameters, successful status and raw-byte digest. Reject any unmatched, duplicate, extra or uncertain motion.
2. Under fresh exclusive ownership, independently force and verify output OFF and matrix open, inspect errors, and read actual stage/XY. Require the exact expected final position; do not issue corrective motion to make the narrative fit.
3. Preserve the interrupted prefix and a separate recovery assessment. A finalization path must be scoped to the exact returned-motion prefix and permit only the missing readbacks and electrical cleanup. Rebinding an abandoned owner claim requires an explicit audited recovery mechanism; do not casually populate private claim fields or weaken normal reservation guards.
4. Perform fresh schema discovery before using the native capture adapter. Append only missing final-position and cleanup evidence, then mark navigation complete only after the full normal native-history validator accepts the result.
5. Complete the actual image checkpoint and fresh acquisition readiness. Verify native acquisition started; a running process alone is insufficient.

The exercised recovery finalized missing post-Y/cleanup evidence without repeating retract, X or Y travel; the next device then completed its once-only preapproach and began forming. This does not establish a generic automatic resume API.

## Execution and reporting

Use a tracked background process or adequate timeout for multi-stage hardware orchestration, including expensive repeated historical audits. A wrapper timeout is not an instrument failure or proof of cleanup. Preserve real tool-approval boundaries even when routine campaign authorization is already present.

Under standing continuous-campaign authorization, do routine terminal reconciliation and advance to the next saved assignment without repeated authorization prompts. Never extrapolate that authorization into replaying stress, ambiguous motion, or bypassing a safety gate. Report actual phase/run ID and preserve historical flags explicitly.
